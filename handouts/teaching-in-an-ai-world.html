Slides 6–7

"Ubiquitous AI" and a duty of care

AI is increasingly part of the environment students learn in, not only a separate tool they choose to open. Different ages and learning goals call for different relationships with it.

With AIUse it when it can extend learning, feedback, access, comparison, creation, or inquiry.
Without AIProtect the practice students need to build knowledge, attention, fluency, and independent capacity.
About AIHelp students understand what AI is doing, how to evaluate it, and what responsibilities stay with them.

Slides 8–9

AI literacy and AI fluency

AI literacyUnderstand · Evaluate · Question · Decide. "Do I understand enough to make a good judgment?"
AI fluencyDelegate · Describe · Discern · Use responsibly. "Can I work effectively with the system when I choose to use it?"

Everyone needs literacy. The level of fluency can vary by age, purpose, and context. Fluent output still needs evaluation: search can improve grounding, but it does not remove the need for judgment.

Slide 10

Ask AI about something you know well

Try it now · about 1 minute

Pick your subject, a hobby, your hometown, or a process you do at work. Choose something where you can actually judge the answer, not something obscure meant to make it fail.

Then turn to a neighbor: one thing the answer did reasonably well, and one thing you noticed only because you knew the subject.

Slides 11–14, 16–17

Small classroom moves

You don't need a new AI unit. As you go, ask whether one of these fits something you already teach.

Be the student

Evaluate an AI response together

The Dust Bowl was caused by a severe drought across the Great Plains in the 1930s. With little rain, the soil dried out and strong winds created massive dust storms.
What students often notice

Drought and wind are true but incomplete. Land-use practices, farming methods, and economic context matter too. The habit to build is asking what is missing and what evidence would help decide, not only catching a false statement.

Students don't need individual AI accounts for this activity.

Planner

Student first, AI later

Where does the student need the practice? Protect that step. AI can enter afterward as a comparison, challenger, source of alternatives, or feedback partner.

Ask AI stepAsk AI the same research question, compare with your sources, mark what's accurate, questionable, or missing, and explain what you'd trust and why.
Short reflectionWhat was difficult? What decisions did you make? What did you change, and why? Process work should reveal learning, not become paperwork.

The same discernment applies to us

AI is being built directly into planning, materials, differentiation, feedback, and repeated tasks. A product menu is not a pedagogical argument. Testing a tool is not the same as adopting it.

Slide 25

Ask AI to help, after students think
